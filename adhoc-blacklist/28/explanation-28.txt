After eliminating the triangle 8'10'7 the face 38769'12'4 becomes a {6,1} hexagon with the obtuse angle in common with the quadrilateral 4'12'90. At this point if we remove the edges 4'12' and '12'9 we have formed an hexagon {6,2} of the type which we know does not exist (see Special Hexagon Lemma) if all its edges are strictly shorter than \pi. We need therefore to check that, after the removal of the triangle, the hexagon {6,1} which is newly formed has no edges of length >=\pi. Four of the edges of this hexagon would be in common with a convex face. The remaining two are the edges connecting 2 and 6 to the baricenter of the triangle. The baricenter of the triangle would be a concave angle of the adjacent face to the newly formed hexagon. The long edge lemma implies that the latter face would need to have normalized area at least 5, if any of these edges were long \pi or more. However such face would be a {6,2} hexagon, which has area 4.
