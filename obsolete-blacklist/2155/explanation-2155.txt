There is a nonconvex pentagon with two consecutive concave angles.
In order to apply the argument to exclude two consecutive concave angles, recall
that we need to show, in the difficult case, that the edges are no longer than pi.
However the argument in the overleaf already cover the edges which connect to a concave
angle. Thus we need to show that the edges 10 and 13 are both no longer than \pi.
Note that 10 is because it is in common with a convex triangle. Next look at
the face 041523. It is an hexagon with 2 concave angles, so it must have area 4\pi/3. 
Now assume that 13 is long more than \pi. Add the edges of the original net O so
to create a pin P in the face 041523. Take the antipodal of 3 in the edge 13 and
call it A. While keeping 3 and A fixed, rotate the halfequator 3A inwards
in the face 041523 until it encouters another point in the net O. This happens when
the halfequator hits the pin. But because the pin is connected with an edge to 
the vertex 3, the halfequator has completed a rotation of 2\pi/3, sweeping
an area of 4\pi/3. Because the face 041523 has area 4\pi/3, the sector swept by
the halfequator and the face have to coincide, which is not possible.
