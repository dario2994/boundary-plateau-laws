The two convex triangles must be the two equilateral triangles in a regular tetrahedral net.
The remaining nonconvex quadrilateral is precisely the region obtained by taking the union
of the two other equilateral triangle in the regular net and removing their common edge E. The
pin must therefore be contained on this removed edge. A necessary condition is that the antipode
of the pin is supported in the net. This happens if and only if the pin is in the midpoint of
E. Its antipode is however the midpoint of the "opposite" edge in the tetrahedron (the only edge 
with no points in common with E). On the other hand if this were the original net, then we would
need to remove both pins to get the derived net: the derived net would consist of a loop subdividing 
the sphere in two faces, but this would then be an extinct net.
